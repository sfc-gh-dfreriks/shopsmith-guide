# Belt sander (6" × 48")

The belt runs in one direction, so it sands **with the grain without swirl marks**. It handles faces, edges, ends, miters, bevels, curves and tool sharpening. You can use it **horizontal or vertical**. [Belt Sanding p.1–2]

## Capacities

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Belt | 6" × 48", coarse / medium / fine; automatic tension [Belt Sanding p.3] |
| Hard side (over the backup plate) | 6" × 14" with the table as a backstop; 6" × 16-1/2" without [Belt Sanding p.1] |
| Soft side (no plate) | 3" × 16"; use only the center 3" [Belt Sanding p.1] |
| Worktable | 6" × 9"; tilts 0–20° toward the belt or 0–45° away; has a miter slot [Belt Sanding p.1] |

## Parts of the belt sander

- **Drive drum**: rubber-sleeved, and drives the belt. [Belt Sanding p.1]
- **Idler drum**: spring-loaded, and tensions the belt automatically. [Belt Sanding p.1]
- **Tension knob** (left side of the idler drum): releases tension so you can change belts. [Belt Sanding p.1]
- **Tracking knob** (behind the tension knob): tilts the idler so the belt runs centered. [Belt Sanding p.1]
- **Backup plate**: a flat steel plate behind the "hard side". [Belt Sanding p.1]
- **Worktable** with **tilt lock**: mounts in 4 positions. [Belt Sanding p.1, p.13]
- **Sander support**, **trunnion** and **trunnion lock**, plus the **mounting tubes** that fit the Mark V Power Mount. [BS Dust Chute p.1]
- **Auxiliary spindle and cap**, and the **dust chute** in the lower casting. [Belt Sanding p.2]

## Mount it on the Mark V

The belt sander mounts in the **Power Mount** (the left end of the headstock). It is driven by the **upper auxiliary spindle** and held by the **accessory mount locks**. [520S Owner's Manual p.19; BS Dust Chute p.1]

> Full step-by-step coupling instructions are in the **Belt Sander Owner's Manual**, which isn't in your collection. [Belt Sanding p.1] The steps below are the general Power Mount pattern. Check them against that manual.

- [ ] Unplug the Mark V. Set the speed dial to **Slow** the next time it runs.
- [ ] Remove the spindle cap from the upper auxiliary spindle if one is fitted. [Belt Sanding p.2]
- [ ] Slide the sander's **mounting tubes** into the Power Mount holes, engaging the drive with the upper auxiliary spindle. [520S Owner's Manual p.19]
- [ ] Tighten the **accessory mount locks**. [520S Owner's Manual p.19]
- [ ] Set the sander **fully horizontal or fully vertical** with the trunnion and lock it. Avoid angles in between unless the extra bolt is fitted. [Belt Sanding p.4]
- [ ] Mount the worktable and lock it with its edge **no more than 1/16" above the belt, but not touching it**. [Belt Sanding p.4]

## Before every use

- [ ] The belt tracks centered and touches nothing. [Belt Sanding p.4]
- [ ] The table is locked, within 1/16" of the belt, and **never tilted toward the belt**. [Belt Sanding p.4]
- [ ] There are no wrenches or parts on the belt. [Belt Sanding p.4]
- [ ] The dust hose is connected, or you are wearing a mask. [Belt Sanding p.4]
- [ ] Start the machine, **then** set the speed. Bring the work to the belt only after it's up to speed. [Belt Sanding p.4–5]

## Speed

[Belt Sanding p.5]

| Sanding area | Hardwood | Softwood |
|---|---|---|
| Large | E (1150 RPM) | F (1300 RPM) |
| Medium | G (1450 RPM) | H (1600 RPM) |
| Small | H (1600 RPM) | J (1900 RPM) |
| Grinding or sharpening metal | Slow (700 RPM) | Slow (700 RPM) |

Lower speeds give more torque for big surfaces. [Belt Sanding p.5]

## Your first sanding job: a board face

- [ ] Set the sander horizontal, with the table straddling or parallel to the belt, 1/16" above it. [Belt Sanding p.5–7]
- [ ] Set speed **E** for hardwood or **F** for softwood (large area). [Belt Sanding p.5]
- [ ] Hold the work flat, **sand with the grain**, use only enough pressure to hold it, and **keep it moving** so it doesn't burn. [Belt Sanding p.5–6]
- [ ] For work shorter than 14", use the table as a backstop. For longer work, set the table parallel to the belt. [Belt Sanding p.6]
- [ ] Work in the **center** of the belt. Sanding at the edges makes the belt wander and fray. [Belt Sanding p.12]

## Other operations

- **Fast stock removal:** sand across the grain, then finish with the grain. [Belt Sanding p.6]
- **Edges:** keep the work moving evenly end to end so you don't sand a hollow in the middle. [Belt Sanding p.7]
- **End grain:** lock the miter gauge in the table slot and check it's 90° to the belt. Press the end straight in; don't slide it. [Belt Sanding p.8–9]
- **Miters, bevels and compounds:**
  - For a miter, set the miter gauge.
  - For a bevel, tilt the table **away from the belt only**.
  - For a compound angle, set both.

  [Belt Sanding p.9–10]
- **Outside curves:** rock the work on the hard side. **Inside curves:** use light pressure on the idler drum. **Compound curves:** use the soft side, vertical, with no table, in the center 3" only. [Belt Sanding p.11–12]
- **Sharpening tools:** set the sander vertical with the table at 0. Use the Sharpening Guide in front of the backup plate, run at **Slow**, and **clean out all sawdust first**: sparks ignite it. [Sharpening p.4; Belt Sanding p.5]

## Change a belt and track it

- [ ] Unplug. Release the belt with the **tension knob**. [Belt Sanding p.1]
- [ ] Slide off the old belt. Fit the new one with the arrow (if printed) matching the direction of rotation. *(Arrow direction isn't stated in your manuals; check the belt's label.)*
- [ ] Re-engage the tension knob. A standard 6" × 48" belt needs no drum readjustment. [Belt Sanding p.3]
- [ ] Plug in and run it briefly. Turn the **tracking knob** until the belt runs centered on the backup plate. Tracking and crowning are the **only** adjustments allowed while plugged in. [Belt Sanding p.1, p.5]

## Care and safety

- Clean belts by holding an abrasive cleaning stick against the running belt. [Belt Sanding p.12]
- Store belts flat and clean, so they don't kink. Throw out worn belts. [Belt Sanding p.3–4]
- Heavy pressure overheats and can **warp the backup plate**, and pressing hard on the idler can ruin the belt's crown. Once the crown is gone, the belt won't track. [Belt Sanding p.5–6, p.11]
- **Danger zone:** 3" around the belt, plus 6' behind it with the table parallel or removed. Never stand in line with the belt's travel. [Belt Sanding p.3]
- Hold small parts with taped pliers or a fixture. Never start with work on the belt, and never spin the belt by hand. [Belt Sanding p.4]
- Don't sand lead paint or particle board. Don't put a heavy dust collector on the same circuit as the Mark V. [Belt Sanding p.3–4]
- **Never use dust collection while grinding metal.** [Knife Jig p.3]

## Dust chute upgrade (300002)

The upgrade fits a 2-1/2" hose. [BS Dust Chute p.1–2]

- [ ] Unplug. Lay the sander horizontal with the mounting tubes up.
- [ ] Remove the trunnion lock nut (9/16" socket), noting the order of the hardware.
- [ ] Mount the bare sander support on the Mark V and lock it. File off the bump on its curved top, without touching the trunnion track.
- [ ] Reassemble in the original order. Remove the old chute (two socket-head screws, 1/4" Allen wrench).
- [ ] Fit the new chute on the right frame side with 1/4-20 × 1" screws and jam nuts. Point the elbow down and connect the hose.
