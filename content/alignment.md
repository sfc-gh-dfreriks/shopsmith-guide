# Alignment & maintenance

A machine that's been moved, or just bought used, should be checked before its first real cut. [520 Summary p.9]

## Before you start

- [ ] Set the dial to **Slow**, switch off and **unplug**. [520 Summary p.9]
- [ ] Gather your tools:
  - Allen wrenches: 3/16", 5/32" (long and short), and 5/16".
  - Wrenches: 9/16", 1/2", and a 1/2" socket with extension.
  - The arbor wrench.
  - A straight 3/8"–1/2" drill bit, screwdrivers, and an accurate combination square.

  [520 Summary p.9]
- [ ] **Check your square.** Draw a line along a true edge, flip the square over, and draw again. The two lines must be parallel. [520 Summary p.9]
- [ ] Retract the casters and set the legs firmly on a level floor. [520 Summary p.9]

The saw blade stands in for the spindle's plane of rotation in these checks. Keep the square **off the teeth**. [520 Summary p.9, p.12]

## Alignment sequence (Model 520)

Work through these in order. Each step depends on the one before it.

- [ ] **1. Headrest lock.** Lift the tubes near the tie bar.
  - If there's give, unlock it and turn the lock shaft clockwise with a Phillips screwdriver.
  - If it's too stiff, turn the shaft counter-clockwise.

  [520 Summary p.9]
- [ ] **2. Carriage lock.** Adjust the nut at the back of the carriage (1/2" socket) until the handle locks **horizontal** and the carriage won't move. Then add **1/4 turn**. [520 Summary p.9–10]
- [ ] **3. 90° table stops.**
  1. Raise the machine vertical and tilt the table 90°.
  2. Chuck a straight 3/8"–1/2" bit and extend it 1/2" into the table opening.
  3. Adjust until the square touches the bit along its full length, then lock the table.
  4. Turn both 90° stop bolts (1/2" wrench) until they touch the underside of the table.
  5. Recheck.

  [520 Summary p.10–11]
- [ ] **4. 0° stop.** Lower the machine and mount the blade. Square the table to the blade. Press in the stop pin and adjust the stop bolt until it just touches the pin. [520 Summary p.12–13]
- [ ] **5. Tilt indicator.** At 0°, loosen the two Phillips screws and line up the "0" marks. [520 Summary p.13]
- [ ] **6. 45° stops.** Center the blade under the insert slot using the quill. Set the table to exactly 45° with the square, then adjust the front and rear 45° stop bolts. [520 Summary p.14]
- [ ] **7. Miter slots parallel to the blade.**
  1. Clamp the long 5/32" Allen wrench in the miter gauge so its tip touches the blade body, not a tooth.
  2. Slide the gauge front to back.
  3. If the tip binds or a gap opens: tilt the table to 45° and loosen the four 5/16" trunnion bolts.
  4. Return to 0° and adjust until the tip touches evenly.
  5. Tighten the two bolts nearest the headstock, tilt to 45°, and tighten the other two.
  6. **Lock the tilt before tightening**, or the table bows.

  [520 Summary p.15]
- [ ] **8. Table insert.** Tighten the rear screw first, then draw the front flush. [520 Summary p.16]
- [ ] **9. Miter gauge glides.** Set them so the gauge rides **1/64"–1/32"** off the table without rocking. [520 Summary p.16]
- [ ] **10. Miter gauge face and stops.**
  1. Square the face to the blade and set the pointer to 0.
  2. For each stop (90° and both 45°): back it out 2–3 turns, press the plunger, and turn the stop in until it just touches.

  [520 Summary p.16–17]
- [ ] **11. Pro Fence parallel.**
  1. Put the miter gauge in the left slot with the Allen wrench as a feeler, and bring the fence up to touch its tip.
  2. Lock the **lower** handle first, then the upper.
  3. If the tip pulls away or binds as you slide the gauge: loosen the four button-head screws, unlock the upper handle only, adjust the rear of the fence, then relock and retighten.

  [520 Summary p.17]
- [ ] **12. Extension table.** Mount it in the base mount and loosen the bottom nuts about 1/4". Level it to the main table. [520 Summary p.18]
  - *Older-model shim method:* use .030" and .005" shims under the table. Tighten the tube nuts until they bottom out, then **no more than 1/2 turn** more. [Alignment & Maintenance p.8–9]
- [ ] **13. Riving knife.** Seat it fully, about **1/8"** from the teeth and centered on the blade.
  - If it sits to the left, back the collar stop screw out.
  - If it sits to the right, screw it in.

  [520 Summary p.18–19]
- [ ] **14. Lathe centers.** Close the chuck jaws and extend the quill until the cup center point meets the jaws.
  - **Horizontal:** loosen the setscrew and rotate the tailstock's adjustable center.
  - **Vertical:** reposition the tailstock using its stop collars.

  [520 Summary p.19]

## Maintenance schedule

Running time is about **20% of shop time**, so 25 shop hours is roughly 4–6 running hours. **Do the 50-hour items at least once a year.** [520 Summary p.31]

| Interval (running hours) | Tasks |
|---|---|
| As needed | Clean the guards and sanding disc. Sharpen blades, bits and chisels. |
| Every 5 | Clean and wax the machine. |
| Every 10 | Everything above, plus:<br>• Lubricate the drive shaft and idler shaft.<br>• Check the anti-kickback system and lower guard.<br>• Wax the riving knife.<br>• Lubricate the chuck.<br>• Recheck alignment steps 1–14. |
| Every 25, or yearly | Lubricate the sheaves. |
| Every 50, or yearly | Everything above, plus lubricate the headstock lock. |

Source: [520 Summary p.31]. *The manuals don't describe how to lubricate the drive shaft and idler shaft. Ask Shopsmith.*

## How to do each task

### Clean (every 5 hours or monthly)

[520 Summary p.32]

- [ ] Remove the two belt-cover screws and slide the cover left. Remove the nameplate's bottom screw and rotate the nameplate 180°.
- [ ] Blow out the headstock with compressed air.
- [ ] Clean the miter slots with mineral spirits. Remove the insert and scrape out the sawdust underneath.
- [ ] Brush the quill rack, the table-tube racks and the carriage pinions.

### Wax

[520 Summary p.32; 520S Owner's Manual p.89]

- [ ] Clean with denatured alcohol and file off any burrs.
- [ ] Wax the following with **paste floor or furniture wax** (not car wax or spray polish), then buff:
  - table and extension
  - way tubes
  - rip fence and miter bar
  - quill and mounting holes
- **Don't wax the trunnions or the racks.** Buff the wax before you slide the headstock or carriage over it.

### Oil the sheaves

[520S Owner's Manual p.88; Alignment & Maintenance p.17–18]

- [ ] Run the dial to its **highest** speed, then switch off and unplug.
- [ ] Turn the spindle by hand until the hole in the **control sheave** shows. Oil it through the nameplate opening.
- [ ] Find the **floating-sheave** hole under the spring, spread the coils with a screwdriver, and oil it.
- [ ] Run the machine through the full speed range several times.
- **Oil:** non-detergent motor oil, a few drops. The manuals differ on the details:
  - 520S manual: 10W, 2–5 drops.
  - Older manual: 30 wt, 1–2 drops.
- **Never use penetrating or aerosol oils.**

### Headstock lock (every 50 hours or yearly)

Loosen it fully and apply powdered graphite or a dry lube where the threaded rod enters the wedge locks. [520S Owner's Manual p.88]

### Drive belt tension

The belt needs tensioning if it deflects **more than 1/8"** under finger pressure (520S manual), or 1/4" (older manual). [520S Owner's Manual p.89; Alignment & Maintenance p.19]

To tension it, loosen the idler-shaft eccentric bushing bolt, turn the bushing clockwise until it stops, and retighten. If the belt is tight but still slips, use belt dressing rather than more tension. [520S Owner's Manual p.89–90]

### Guards, chuck and disc

[520S Owner's Manual p.90]

- **Guards:** clean with denatured alcohol, **never lacquer thinner**. If they get stiff, use graphite, never oil.
- **Chuck:** put a little powdered graphite inside every 10 hours.
- **Sanding disc:** clean it with an abrasive cleaning stick at low speed.

### Storage

Spray bare steel with a rust-inhibiting light oil. Before using the machine again, clean it off with mineral spirits and re-wax. [Alignment & Maintenance p.20]
