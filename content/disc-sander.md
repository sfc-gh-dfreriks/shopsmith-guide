# Disc sander

Use it for end grain, edges, angles, chamfers, and sanding to an exact width with the rip fence. [Disc Sanding p.1–10]

## Set up (from table saw mode)

- [ ] Set the dial to **Slow**, switch off and **unplug**. Remove the upper guard and the blade. [520 Summary p.21–22]
- [ ] **Stick on the sandpaper.** On a brand-new disc, first wipe off the protective oil with denatured alcohol. Press the paper on and smooth it from the center out. [520 Summary p.22]
  - *Old paper peels off. Warming the disc to no more than 150° helps.* [520 Summary p.22]
- [ ] Mount the **lower guard** and open it (cover to the right). [520 Summary p.22]
- [ ] Push the disc fully onto the spindle and tighten the setscrew on the flat. Move the guard cover to **1"** from the disc. [520 Summary p.22]
- [ ] Set the table edge **1/16"** from the disc, or **1/2"** if you'll feed the disc with the quill. Put the table surface **slightly above the disc center**. [520 Summary p.22]
- [ ] Lock check. Keep the quill locked unless you're using quill feed. [520 Summary p.22]

**Speed:** [520 Summary p.26]
- **Coarse 60#:** D (1050) for hardwood, E (1150) for softwood.
- **Medium 80#:** F (1300) / G (1450).
- **Fine 100#:** G (1450) / H (1600).
- **Grinding metal tools:** Slow.

Go a little slower on end grain.

## First job: square up a board end

- [ ] Put the miter gauge in the **left** slot so the stock meets the **downward-moving** side of the disc. Lock it at 90°. [520 Summary p.26]
- [ ] Switch on and set the speed. [520 Summary p.26]
- [ ] Feed the end into the disc for a few seconds, back off, and repeat. *Or* hold the stock and advance the disc with the quill feed. [520 Summary p.26]

## Common mistakes

- Sanding on the **upward** side of the disc. It lifts the work out of your hands. [Disc Sanding p.3]
- Starting the machine with the stock already touching the disc. [Disc Sanding p.3]
- Sanding without the table under the work, or reaching under the table. [Disc Sanding p.2–3]
- End-sanding 3/4" stock wider than 5-1/2". [Disc Sanding p.3]
- Too much speed or pressure, which burns the work. Hesitating mid-pass sands a flat. [Disc Sanding p.3–4, p.10]
- Changing the table or miter angles between sawing a miter and sanding it. Keep the saw settings. [Disc Sanding p.8–9]
