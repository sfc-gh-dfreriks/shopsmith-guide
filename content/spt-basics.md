# Special-purpose tool basics (power take-off)

The Planer and Belt Sander are **special-purpose tools (SPTs)**. They don't use the main spindle the way the five modes do. Instead they mount on the left end of the machine and are driven from the back of the headstock.

## How SPTs are powered

- The Mark V has three spindles: the **main spindle** in the quill, plus an **upper** and a **lower auxiliary spindle**. [520S Owner's Manual p.19]
- The **upper auxiliary spindle** drives the Bandsaw, **Belt Sander**, Strip Sander and **Thickness Planer**. The lower one drives the Jointer and Scroll Saw. [520S Owner's Manual p.19]
- The **Power Mount** on the left side of the headstock carries the extension table and the major SPTs. The **accessory mount locks** clamp whatever is mounted there. [520S Owner's Manual p.19]
- The speed dial sets both the main and upper auxiliary spindles (700–5,200 RPM). The lower auxiliary spindle runs 1.6× faster (1,120–8,320 RPM). [520S Owner's Manual p.22]
- Power reaches the auxiliary spindles through the Poly-V belt and the **drive sleeve assembly**. If the motor and auxiliary spindles run but the main spindle doesn't, suspect the drive and ring assembly. [520S Owner's Manual p.25, p.99]

## Rules for every SPT

- [ ] Only turn the speed dial **while the machine is running**. Return it to **Slow** before switching off. [520S Owner's Manual p.19]
- [ ] Unplug the machine before mounting, unmounting or adjusting any SPT.
- [ ] Check that the accessory mount locks are tight before starting.
- [ ] If two tools are mounted at once (e.g. disc sander plus belt sander), **never exceed the speed of the slowest tool**. [Belt Sanding p.2]
- [ ] Fit the spindle cap on any auxiliary spindle that isn't in use. [Belt Sanding p.2]
