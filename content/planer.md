# Planer (12" Thickness Planer)

The planer **smooths a board's faces and brings boards to exactly the same thickness**. It does **not** straighten cupped or warped boards; flatten those on a jointer first. [Thickness Planing p.1]

## Capacities

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Stock thickness | 3/32" to 4" [Thickness Planing p.2] |
| Stock width | 1" to 12" [Thickness Planing p.2] |
| Minimum length | 12" [Thickness Planing p.3] |
| Maximum depth of cut | 3/32" (most work needs less) [Thickness Planing p.3] |
| Cutterhead | 3 knives, 12-1/4" long; 2-1/4" diameter with knives [Thickness Planing p.2] |
| Table | 28" × 12-1/2" on 4 threaded posts [Thickness Planing p.2] |
| Feed motor | 1/20 hp, variable speed [Thickness Planing p.2] |
| Mark V speed | **"T" (4100 RPM)** [Thickness Planing p.8] |

## Parts of the planer

- **Cutterhead and knives.** There are 3 wedge-shaped knives. Each is clamped by 2 wedges with 4 locking screws per wedge. [Thickness Planing p.2]
- **Knife leveling screws**: 3 per knife. They set all the knives to the same height. [Thickness Planing p.2]
- **Infeed and outfeed shields**: these must be locked down whenever the planer runs. [Thickness Planing p.2–3]
- **Infeed and outfeed rollers**: these power-feed the board. [Thickness Planing p.3]
- **Anti-kickback fingers**: these are why stock must be at least 1" wide. [Thickness Planing p.3]
- **Thickness crank** with **scale** and **depth-of-cut pointer**. One turn equals 1/16". [Thickness Planing p.4]
- **Feed control knob**: clockwise is faster. [Thickness Planing p.5]
- **Switch / control box**: runs the feed motor. [PowerPro Planer p.3–4]
- **Drive hub and coupler**: connect the cutterhead shaft to the Mark V. [PowerPro Planer p.2]
- **Way tube clamps** (2): hold the planer on the way tubes. [PowerPro Planer p.2]
- **Logo cover** on the outfeed shield: rotate it to open the dust port. [Planer Dust Chute p.1]

## Mount it on the Mark V

> **Read this first.** The only step-by-step planer mounting sheet in your manuals is written for the **PowerPro** headstock. On a standard Mark V 520, the planer is driven by the **upper auxiliary spindle** [520S Owner's Manual p.19]. The PowerPro sheet tells owners to *move* the drive hub from the upper auxiliary spindle to the quill, which suggests that on your machine the hub **stays on the upper auxiliary spindle** [PowerPro Planer p.1]. That last point is an inference. Confirm it with the Planer Owner's Manual or Shopsmith support before your first run.

- [ ] Unplug the Mark V. Remove everything except the headstock and carriage from the way tubes. [PowerPro Planer p.1]
- [ ] Slide the headstock and carriage to the **left** end of the way tubes. [PowerPro Planer p.2]
- [ ] Set the planer on the way tubes, with the **switch box at the far right** and the **infeed side facing the front** (switch side) of the headstock. [PowerPro Planer p.2]
- [ ] Slide the planer to the far right end and tighten both **way tube clamps**. [PowerPro Planer p.2]
- [ ] Slip the **coupler** onto the planer's drive hub. [PowerPro Planer p.2]
- [ ] Slide the headstock toward the planer until the drive end is within **1/2"** of the coupler. Engage the coupler on the **upper auxiliary spindle** (verify with the Planer manual). Lock the headstock. [PowerPro Planer p.2; 520S Owner's Manual p.19]
- [ ] Check that the shields are down and locked, and that no tools are on the table.
- [ ] Plug in the Mark V and the planer control box. *(The Mark V wiring isn't shown in your manuals; follow the Planer manual.)*

> The PowerPro sheet warns that a PowerPro motor must run in **REVERSE** to drive the planer from the quill. **That doesn't apply to a standard Mark V**, which has no direction switch. [PowerPro Planer p.3–4]

## Setting speed and feed

- [ ] Start the Mark V and **set the speed dial to "T" (4100 RPM)** while it runs. [Thickness Planing p.8]
- Harder wood, wider boards, deeper cuts and duller knives all need a slower cutterhead and feed. [Thickness Planing p.4]
- If it bogs down, reduce in this order: **feed rate**, then **depth of cut**, then **cutterhead speed**. [Thickness Planing p.5]
- For a glass-smooth last pass, take 1/64" or less, raise the speed 1–2 letters, and set the feed to SLOW. [Thickness Planing p.9]

**Feed rate chart** in feet per minute (hardwood / softwood), for clear, straight-grained wood. Use lower settings for knots or figured grain. [Thickness Planing p.8]

| Depth of cut | 0–4" wide | 4–8" wide | 8–12" wide |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/64" | 10 / 10 | 10 / 10 | 10 / 10 |
| 1/32" | 10 / 10 | 10 / 10 | 9 / 7 |
| 1/16" | 10 / 10 | 5 / 5 | not recommended |
| 3/32" | 6 / 9 | not recommended | not recommended |

## Your first board

- [ ] Joint out any cup or warp first. On rough lumber, joint one edge so you can read the grain. [Thickness Planing p.1, p.13]
- [ ] Check the board: dry and clean, with **no nails, staples, paint, dirt or glue beads**. [Thickness Planing p.3, p.12]
- [ ] Measure the board at its **thickest** point. Set the pointer to exactly that. **The first pass is always at "0" depth.** [Thickness Planing p.9]
- [ ] Always set the height from thick to thin. Lower the table at least one full crank turn past the mark, then raise it to the mark. This stops the table drifting and tapering your board. [Thickness Planing p.4]
- [ ] Set up a helper or roller stands at exact table height for boards longer than a couple of feet. [Thickness Planing p.9]
- [ ] Stand to the **side** of the infeed opening, near the switch. [Thickness Planing p.9]
- [ ] Start the planer and let it reach full speed. Feed the board **with the grain**, parallel to the table sides, until the infeed roller grabs it. Then just support it; don't push or pull. [Thickness Planing p.9, p.11]
- [ ] Move to the outfeed side, to the right of the opening, and support the board as it comes out. [Thickness Planing p.10]
- [ ] Raise the table about one crank turn (1/16") or less, and repeat. [Thickness Planing p.4, p.11]
- [ ] Plane **both faces**. Take off one side's saw marks, flip the board, then plane to final thickness. [Thickness Planing p.14]

**Tips:**
- **Several boards to one thickness:** plane the thickest down to the next-thickest, then run both, and so on. Feed one board at a time, never side by side. [Thickness Planing p.14]
- **Knots and wild grain:** take 1/128"–1/64" at a slow feed. [Thickness Planing p.12]
- **Chip-out** means you're feeding against the grain. Flip the board end-for-end. [Thickness Planing p.11]
- **Glue-ups:** let them dry 24 hours and scrape off any glue first. [Thickness Planing p.15]

## Never

- Never run without the shields in place and locked. [Thickness Planing p.3]
- Never reach under a shield. Switch off, unplug and wait for a full stop, then clear it with a **wooden or plastic stick**. [Thickness Planing p.3]
- Never plane plywood, particle board, painted or varnished or secondhand lumber, loose knots, or stock under 1" wide, over 12" wide or under 12" long. Never plane on edge. [Thickness Planing p.3]
- Never start it with a board under the cutterhead, and never turn the crank while it's cutting. [Thickness Planing p.4, p.9]
- Always wear eye and ear protection and a dust mask. The **danger zone** includes 6' in front of and behind the board. [Thickness Planing p.2]

## Knives

- Use only **Shopsmith wedge-type planer knives**. [Thickness Planing p.2]
- [ ] **Every 10 hours of use**, check the wedge locking screws. Check them right away if you notice noise, vibration or uneven cuts. [Thickness Planing p.2]
- Knife removal, installation and height setting with the leveling screws are in the **Planer Owner's Manual** (not in your collection). [Sharpening p.17]
- **Sharpening with the Knife Sharpening Jig (555471)** uses the Conical Sanding Disc at **Slow**:
  - Remove the sawdust and line the lower saw guard with foil.
  - **Don't use dust collection while grinding metal**: sparks can ignite the dust.
  - Clamp the knife edge-up in the jig, lock the rip fence behind it, and feed left-to-right only until the sparks stop.
  - Over a knife's life, remove no more than 1/16" in total. Retire knives narrower than 11/16".

  [Knife Jig p.3–8]

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|
| Boards hesitate or stick | Clean the rollers with a damp cloth, then paste-wax and buff the table. [Thickness Planing p.10] |
| Snipe at the ends | Under 0.005" is normal. Otherwise support the board level as it enters and exits. If snipe persists, check the roller springs (see the Planer manual). [Thickness Planing p.12] |
| Long ridges along the board | Nicked knives: regrind them. [Thickness Planing p.12] |
| Ripples across the board (mill marks) | Raise the cutterhead speed or slow the feed, then check the knife heights. [Thickness Planing p.12] |

## Dust chute (300001 / retrofit 555643)

- [ ] Unplug. Remove the screw in the logo on the outfeed shield, rotate the logo 180° to open the port, and refit the screw. [Planer Dust Chute p.1]
- [ ] Clip the metal chute bottom to the plastic chute with the 4 clips. [Planer Dust Chute p.2]
- [ ] Raise the outfeed shield. Seat the chute's outlet tab on the outfeed tie bar and the wing tabs on the side castings, then press until it pops in. [Planer Dust Chute p.2]
- [ ] Lower and lock the shield. Fit the 2-1/2" elbow and hose. [Planer Dust Chute p.2]
- [ ] **Always start the dust collector before the planer.** With the chute fitted and no suction, it clogs fast. [Planer Dust Chute p.1]
